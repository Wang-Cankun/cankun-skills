## 1. Creative foundation

Build original slow-travel imagery around six ideas:

- **Atmosphere before landmark:** Photograph how a place feels, not just what identifies it.
- **Quiet precision:** Keep the frame calm, but make every edge, line, gap, and object intentional.
- **Environment as collaborator:** Observe existing light, weather, movement, and texture before directing the scene.
- **Architecture as narrative structure:** Use spatial order to hold fleeting human details.
- **Lived-in evidence:** Favor traces of life over staged performance.
- **Raw and refined in balance:** Pair weathered surfaces and ordinary gestures with disciplined framing.

The aim is not rustic nostalgia or generic “Italian summer.” It is a contemporary, observant travel language that gives time, material, and space equal weight.

## 2. Composition

### Spatial grammar

- Use walls, colonnades, windows, doors, terraces, stairs, shorelines, and furniture edges as organizing lines.
- Choose one dominant spatial move per frame: symmetry, long perspective, layered depth, frame-within-frame, diagonal flow, or broad negative space.
- Allow architecture or landscape to occupy most of the image when scale is part of the emotion.
- Place people as anchors rather than automatic centers. A small figure, cropped gesture, reflection, or partial silhouette can be enough.
- Keep verticals deliberate in architectural images. Correct accidental keystoning; retain purposeful perspective.
- Use foreground occlusion sparingly to create discovery: a curtain edge, doorway, foliage, chair back, or shoulder.
- Let emptiness breathe. Do not fill negative space with arbitrary props.

### Frame rhythm

For a sequence, alternate:

- wide place-setter;
- spatial medium shot;
- human action;
- tactile close-up;
- transitional light or movement;
- quiet closing image.

Avoid consecutive frames with the same distance, horizon position, or visual weight.

## 3. Light

- Start with available light. Prefer soft early light, directional window light, late sun, open shade, reflected light, or a plausible low-light practical source.
- Use hard Mediterranean sun when its shadows create architecture; do not flatten it indiscriminately.
- Preserve luminous whites and sunlit plaster. Avoid clipped highlights that erase texture.
- Let shadow retain color and enough information to feel dimensional.
- Backlight fabric, hair, steam, dust, or curtains when motion matters.
- Add artificial light only when it can remain motivated by the scene. Avoid obvious frontal flash unless the user explicitly requests a departure.

## 4. Color and finish

### Palette

Build color from the location:

- chalk, plaster, linen, sand, stone, and warm wood;
- olive, sage, dry grass, caper green, or muted botanical tones;
- sea blue, mineral blue, faded turquoise, or deep sky as controlled accents;
- terracotta, rust, wine, citrus, or sun-faded red in small amounts;
- charcoal, espresso, and natural skin tones for grounding.

Keep a few saturated local colors when the place genuinely contains them. “Restrained” does not mean uniformly beige.

### Grade

- Shift warmth slightly toward the actual light source.
- Protect neutral whites from becoming orange.
- Use moderate contrast with gentle highlight roll-off.
- Keep blacks dimensional rather than crushed.
- Reduce distracting neon color selectively, not with global desaturation.
- Retain skin texture and natural color variation.
- Add fine, restrained grain only when it supports cohesion.
- Keep sharpening natural; avoid crunchy micro-contrast and HDR halos.

## 5. People, objects, and motion

- Direct a real action: crossing a terrace, opening shutters, carrying breakfast, reading by a window, swimming away, setting a table, or pausing in shade.
- Favor side, back, profile, cropped, or in-motion views over repeated camera-facing poses.
- Allow imperfect but meaningful traces: rumpled linen, wet footprints, a half-peeled fruit, an unfinished cup, an open book, moving fabric.
- Use hands and gestures to connect the human story to the place.
- Let wind and water create movement. Use a shutter speed that either preserves a decisive gesture or introduces a controlled trace of motion.
- Keep expressions unperformed. Avoid influencer poses, exaggerated laughter, toe-pointing, and conspicuous product display.

## 6. Location and styling

- Seek local character in ordinary spaces: family cafés, quiet streets, artisan workrooms, courtyards, private homes, markets, ferries, and modest beach structures.
- Prefer material truth: worn stone, limewash, tile, cane, linen, ceramic, timber, metal, glass, salt, and vegetation.
- Choose garments that move and belong: linen, cotton, silk, knitwear, simple swimwear, or structured neutral pieces.
- Integrate bags, hats, books, tableware, food, and flowers only when a person could plausibly have left or used them.
- Avoid over-curated hotel perfection. Introduce one believable sign of presence.
- Do not disclose sensitive or intentionally untagged locations merely to make a shot list more specific.

## 7. Technical translation

Publicly documented working preferences include a Fujifilm camera, manual exposure, RAW capture, shallow depth of field around f/2.8–f/4, and a slight warming of the final image. Translate these as starting principles:

- **Lens:** use 28–35 mm equivalent for environmental space, 50 mm for balanced details and portraits, and 70–90 mm for compressed fragments. Do not overuse ultra-wide distortion.
- **Aperture:** use f/2.8–f/4 for selective depth; stop down when architectural layers must remain legible.
- **Exposure:** protect important highlights and expose skin or key texture intentionally.
- **White balance:** set deliberately or preserve RAW flexibility; do not rely on a global orange preset.
- **Focus:** choose the narrative anchor, not automatically the nearest face.

Lens ranges and exposure guidance above are practical translations, not attributed quotations or fixed rules of the photographer.

## 8. Failure modes

Reject or revise:

- postcard landmarks with no lived detail;
- generic beige “quiet luxury” styling;
- heavy teal-orange grading;
- crushed blacks or chalky lifted blacks without scene logic;
- identical centered compositions across a carousel;
- props arranged too perfectly to feel used;
- models posing more loudly than the location;
- excessive skin smoothing or body reshaping;
- fake sun flare, implausible shadows, synthetic bokeh, or HDR halos;
- prompts that use only the creator's name instead of visible decisions;
- copying the layout, subject, and distinctive details of one existing photograph.

## 9. Prompt vocabulary

Prefer combinations of:

- quiet observational travel photograph;
- architecturally composed;
- sun-warmed plaster with retained texture;
- generous negative space;
- layered doorways or receding thresholds;
- soft early side light;
- hard noon shadow used graphically;
- natural window light;
- restrained mineral and earth palette;
- wind-moved linen;
- unforced human gesture;
- lived-in table or room;
- shallow but plausible depth of field;
- gentle highlight roll-off;
- dimensional shadows;
- fine natural grain;
- understated editorial realism;
- a fleeting in-between moment.
