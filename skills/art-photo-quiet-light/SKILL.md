# Art Photo — Quiet Light

Translate the reference into an original photographic system: atmosphere before spectacle, architecture as the frame, natural light as the stylist, and small human traces as the story.

## Load the references

- Read [references/visual-language.md](references/visual-language.md) for every task.
- Also read [references/shot-recipes.md](references/shot-recipes.md) when planning a shoot, carousel, photo essay, campaign, or multi-image sequence.
- Read [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) only when provenance, attribution, or the evidence behind the style system matters.

## Establish the brief

Identify the subject, location, intended use, aspect ratio, image count, season, time of day, available equipment, and non-negotiable details. Inspect every supplied reference image before proposing edits.

Ask one concise question only when a missing answer would materially change the result. Otherwise state compact assumptions and proceed.

Define the image in one sentence:

`[quiet action or trace] + [place-defining spatial element] + [specific natural light] + [emotional temperature]`

Prefer a lived moment over a generic destination view.

## Build the direction

1. Let the location lead. Inventory lines, thresholds, surfaces, wind, reflections, shadows, and signs of use before adding styling.
2. Choose one architectural idea: symmetry, layered depth, frame-within-frame, long axis, graphic shadow, or generous negative space.
3. Add one human anchor without crowding the frame: a hand, turned figure, unmade bed, half-finished drink, moving curtain, open door, or table awaiting someone.
4. Use clothing and props as evidence of the story. Favor tactile, place-compatible materials and restrained colors; remove decorative objects that merely fill space.
5. Plan a sequence with varied scale and tempo. Do not repeat the same centered wide shot.
6. Grade for believable warmth, retained highlight detail, tactile texture, and coherent color across the set.

## Choose the execution path

### Plan a real shoot

Return:

- a one-sentence creative thesis;
- the best light window and fallback;
- palette, wardrobe, props, and surfaces;
- a numbered shot list with framing, action, lens range, and exposure intent;
- continuity notes for a coherent sequence;
- a restrained post-production recipe.

Use manual exposure and RAW when available. Treat f/2.8–f/4 as a useful starting range, not a mandatory setting. Adapt shutter speed, ISO, and aperture to motion, depth, and the actual light.

### Generate a new image

Load the image-generation skill and tool when available. Convert the named reference into observable traits before sending the prompt: subject, place, composition, light, palette, material texture, gesture, optical behavior, and exclusions.

Do not put the photographer's name or Instagram handle in the generation prompt. Do not recreate a recognizable existing photograph or claim the result is her work. Produce an original image with the requested high-level visual qualities.

### Edit a supplied image

Load the image-generation skill and tool when available. Preserve identity, anatomy, architecture, readable text, object count, and any user-declared invariant unless the user explicitly asks to change it. Describe only the requested changes plus the minimum tonal work needed for cohesion.

### Critique or select images

Separate:

1. direct observations;
2. mismatches against the visual system;
3. the smallest actionable fixes.

Rank images by atmosphere, spatial intelligence, naturalness, narrative detail, technical integrity, and sequence value—not by prettiness alone.

## Write image prompts

Use concrete photographic language. Include:

- the moment and action;
- spatial structure and camera position;
- natural-light direction and quality;
- a restrained, place-specific palette;
- tactile materials and one lived-in detail;
- depth of field, motion, and grain only when they serve the mood;
- exclusions that prevent tourism-ad gloss, excessive staging, orange casts, HDR halos, plastic skin, or synthetic geometry.

Avoid vague phrases such as “luxury aesthetic,” “Instagrammable,” or “cinematic” unless each is unpacked into visible decisions.

## Quality gate

Revise before delivery if any answer is no:

- Does the place feel inhabited rather than displayed?
- Does the composition use space deliberately?
- Is the light plausible and doing narrative work?
- Are styling and props subordinate to the scene?
- Is warmth specific to the light rather than an orange filter?
- Is there at least one tactile or transient detail?
- Does the image remain original rather than trace a known work?
- For a series, do scale, rhythm, and subject emphasis vary?
