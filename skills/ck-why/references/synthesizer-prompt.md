# Synthesizer prompt

Use for a direct or delegated synthesis. Supply the original question, target anchor, investigator findings, and material sources that could not be examined.

Read [epistemics.md](epistemics.md). Answer what the evidence supports about the rationale behind the target, while making the remaining uncertainty legible.

## Synthesis

1. Read the findings, consolidate repeated sources, and distinguish contemporary records from later explanations.
2. Surface conflicting evidence. If the conflict reflects a changed plan or different scopes, explain that with support; otherwise leave it unresolved.
3. Calibrate consequential claims as documented, supported by converging evidence, inferred, speculative, or unknown. Do not treat implementation mechanics as author intent.
4. Spot-check the sources carrying the main conclusion and any citation that looks uncertain. Use read-only access. If a source cannot be checked, retain that limitation.
5. Evaluate the user's hypothesis alongside alternatives. Agreement from another model is not historical evidence.

## Answer

Lead with the best-supported response to the question. Use only the structure needed for the requested depth:

- **Target:** enough file, symbol, parameter, or workflow context to orient the reader.
- **Findings:** documented reasons or converging evidence, with source locations near the relevant claims.
- **Inferences:** the observations and reasoning step, phrased with appropriate uncertainty.
- **Competing hypotheses:** plausible explanations when the record cannot distinguish them, including evidence for and against each.
- **Gaps:** material unanswered questions and the search or access limits behind them.
- **Sources consulted:** a compact account of the meaningful source coverage, including null searches that affect confidence.
- **Confidence:** explain which parts are well established and which remain tentative when that is not already clear.

Confidence labels can help a long or mixed-evidence answer; a clear sentence and citation may suffice for a narrow answer. Do not manufacture a gap or hypothesis to fill a section. Do not omit a consequential gap because it interrupts the narrative.

If a change is being considered, derive a concise set of constraints to preserve, assumptions open to change, known pitfalls, and unresolved risks. Keep these recommendations separate from the historical findings.

## Check before returning

Check the claims that carry the answer: does the evidence support both their substance and their confidence? Did the account preserve contradictions and avoid backfilling intent? Does a null search actually cover the period, source, or version at issue? Distinguish missing coverage from evidence of absence. A reader should be able to follow the strongest citations and know what further inquiry could change the conclusion.
