# Dreyfus Skill Acquisition Model & Heder's AI Fiduciary Framework

Two major intellectual lineages extending Polanyi's tacit knowledge into practical domains: Dreyfus on skill acquisition and the limits of AI, Heder on epistemic opacity and the fiduciary program in the age of autonomous systems.

---

## Part 1: Hubert Dreyfus' Skill Acquisition Model

### 1.1 Origins and Key Works

The Dreyfus model was proposed by brothers **Hubert L. Dreyfus** (philosopher, UC Berkeley) and **Stuart E. Dreyfus** (operations researcher, UC Berkeley) in February 1980, in an 18-page report titled "A Five-Stage Model of the Mental Activities Involved in Directed Skill Acquisition," commissioned by the U.S. Air Force Office of Scientific Research. [Source: Wikipedia, "Dreyfus model of skill acquisition"; Confidence: HIGH]

Key publications:
- **"A Five-Stage Model of the Mental Activities Involved in Directed Skill Acquisition"** (1980) -- original USAF report, UC Berkeley Operations Research Center [Source: ResearchGate listing; Confidence: HIGH]
- **"Mind Over Machine: The Power of Human Intuition and Expertise in the Era of the Computer"** (1986, Free Press) -- Hubert & Stuart Dreyfus. Full elaboration of the five-stage model as a critique of expert systems and symbolic AI [Source: Google Books, Goodreads, Academia.edu; Confidence: HIGH]
- **"What Computers Can't Do: A Critique of Artificial Reason"** (1972, Harper & Row) -- Hubert Dreyfus. First edition of his manifesto against symbolic AI. Revised as "What Computers Still Can't Do" (1992, MIT Press) with new introduction [Source: MIT Press, Amazon; Confidence: HIGH]
- **"The Five-Stage Model of Adult Skill Acquisition"** -- Stuart Dreyfus, *Bulletin of Science, Technology & Society*, Vol. 24, No. 3, pp. 177-181 (2004). Concise summary paper. DOI: 10.1177/0270467604264992 [Source: SAGE Journals, ERIC EJ689871; Confidence: HIGH]
- **"Skillful Coping: Essays on the Phenomenology of Everyday Perception and Action"** (2014, Oxford UP) -- posthumous collection of Hubert Dreyfus's essays on embodied expertise [Source: Oxford University Press; Confidence: HIGH]
- **"Why Heideggerian AI Failed and How Fixing it Would Require Making it More Heideggerian"** (2007) -- *Philosophical Psychology*, Vol. 20, No. 2. Dreyfus's late reassessment of AI and phenomenology [Source: Taylor & Francis; Confidence: HIGH]

### 1.2 The Five Stages -- Detailed Breakdown

Each stage represents a qualitative shift in how the practitioner relates to rules, context, deliberation, and emotional involvement.

#### Stage 1: Novice

- **Rule-following**: Relies entirely on context-free rules and step-by-step instructions
- **Context sensitivity**: None. Operates on abstract, decontextualized features
- **Decision mode**: Deliberate, slow, conscious rule application
- **Emotional stance**: Detached from outcomes; feels no personal responsibility beyond following rules correctly
- **Polanyian mapping**: Pure explicit/focal knowledge. No tacit dimension yet operative. The novice has only "knowing-that" (propositional rules), no "knowing-how"
[Source: Wikipedia Dreyfus model, PMC article 2887319; Confidence: HIGH]

#### Stage 2: Advanced Beginner

- **Rule-following**: Can apply experience-based "maxims" (situational guidelines) beyond rigid rules
- **Context sensitivity**: Begins recognizing situational aspects through direct experience -- "aspectual recognition"
- **Decision mode**: Still analytical, but more sophisticated pattern matching
- **Emotional stance**: Begins feeling engaged; may become overwhelmed by competing demands
- **Polanyian mapping**: First emergence of subsidiary awareness. The practitioner starts to "dwell in" the tool/practice, recognizing patterns not captured by explicit rules
[Source: Wikipedia Dreyfus model; Confidence: HIGH for stage description, MEDIUM for Polanyian mapping -- my synthesis]

#### Stage 3: Competent

- **Rule-following**: Still proceeds by "analysis, calculation, and deliberate rule-following" but selects goals and perspectives consciously
- **Context sensitivity**: Chooses organizing perspective for the situation; filters relevant from irrelevant
- **Decision mode**: Plans deliberately, taking calculated risks about what to attend to
- **Emotional stance**: Heightened emotional involvement -- feels joy at success, regret at failure, because choices are now personal
- **Polanyian mapping**: The "from-to" structure of tacit knowing begins to consolidate. The competent practitioner commits to a focal target and integrates subsidiaries toward it, but the integration is still effortful and conscious
[Source: Wikipedia Dreyfus model; Stuart Dreyfus 2004; Confidence: HIGH for stage, MEDIUM for Polanyian mapping]

#### Stage 4: Proficient

- **Rule-following**: Intuitively grasps situations but still consciously decides on responses
- **Context sensitivity**: "The number of experienced situations is so vast that normally each specific situation immediately dictates an intuitively appropriate action" -- but the proficient practitioner still deliberates about what to do about the intuitive reading
- **Decision mode**: **Critical transition point**: shift "from analytical thinking to intuitive response" in perception, but analytical in action-selection
- **Emotional stance**: Deeply involved; absorbed in the situation
- **Polanyian mapping**: Subsidiary awareness now dominates perception. The practitioner "indwells" the situation (Polanyi's term) -- seeing through the particulars to the comprehensive meaning -- but has not yet fully surrendered deliberate control of action
[Source: Wikipedia, quoting Dreyfus & Dreyfus; Confidence: HIGH for stage, MEDIUM-HIGH for Polanyian mapping]

#### Stage 5: Expert

- **Rule-following**: "Without deliberation or decision-making" -- acts fluidly, automatically, unconsciously
- **Context sensitivity**: Full situational and contextual integration; seamless
- **Decision mode**: "When things are proceeding normally, experts don't solve problems and don't make decisions; they do what normally works" (Dreyfus & Dreyfus, *Mind Over Machine*)
- **Emotional stance**: Fully involved; "reflexive reorientation" when circumstances change abruptly
- **Inability to articulate**: Often cannot explain precisely why they acted as they did
- **Polanyian mapping**: Complete "indwelling." The expert's knowledge is wholly tacit -- the from-to structure is so deeply integrated that the subsidiaries have become entirely transparent. This is Polanyi's paradigm case: "we can know more than we can tell." The expert embodies the skill; it has become an extension of their body
[Source: Wikipedia, Stuart Dreyfus 2004, Mind Over Machine quotes via multiple sources; Confidence: HIGH]

#### Stage 6: Mastery (added later)

- Masters "seek to expand and refine their repertoire of intuitive perspectives" and sometimes "introduce new ways of doing things that open new possibilities and transform the style of their skill domain"
- Examples: Cezanne in painting, Stephen Curry in basketball, B.B. King in music
- **Polanyian mapping**: This maps to Polanyi's concept of "discovery" and "breaking out" -- the creative extension of tacit knowing beyond established patterns. The master does not merely dwell in existing frameworks but generates new ones
[Source: Wikipedia Dreyfus model; Confidence: HIGH for stage, MEDIUM for Polanyian mapping]

### 1.3 Dreyfus's Argument Against AI

Dreyfus identified **four philosophical assumptions** underlying classical AI (GOFAI -- Good Old-Fashioned AI) that he argued were unfounded:

1. **Biological assumption**: That neurons function like Boolean logic gates (on/off switching)
2. **Psychological assumption**: That the mind operates on discrete bits of information via formal rules
3. **Epistemological assumption**: That all knowledge can be formalized as explicit propositions
4. **Ontological assumption**: That reality consists of independent, context-free facts representable by symbols

[Source: Wikipedia, "Hubert Dreyfus's views on artificial intelligence"; Confidence: HIGH]

Dreyfus argued that assumption #3 (the epistemological assumption) directly contradicts Polanyi's tacit knowledge thesis. In his words: "even when we use explicit symbols, we are using them against an unconscious and informal background...without this background our symbols cease to mean anything." [Source: Wikipedia quoting Dreyfus; Confidence: HIGH]

His central claim: **expert knowledge is fundamentally embodied and tacit, therefore it cannot be captured in formal rules or computational algorithms.** This builds directly on Polanyi's "we can know more than we can tell" and Ryle's "knowing-how" vs "knowing-that" distinction (from Ryle's 1945 paper and *The Concept of Mind*, 1949). [Source: Wikipedia tacit knowledge article, Understanding Society blog; Confidence: HIGH]

### 1.4 Phenomenological Foundations

Dreyfus grounded his critique in continental phenomenology:

- **Heidegger**: The distinction between *Zuhandenheit* (ready-to-hand, absorbed skillful use) and *Vorhandenheit* (present-at-hand, detached theoretical contemplation). A hammer ready-to-hand "withdraws" into transparent use; a broken hammer becomes present-at-hand. Expert skill operates in the ready-to-hand mode -- the tool/skill is transparent, not an object of reflection. [Source: Wikipedia, Springer article on skillful coping; Confidence: HIGH]

- **Merleau-Ponty**: The body-subject as the locus of understanding. Knowledge is not "in the head" as representations but "in the body" as habits, dispositions, and motor intentionality. The skilled practitioner's body "knows" what to do before the mind reflects. [Source: Wikipedia Dreyfus AI views, PMC article on reflective interventions; Confidence: HIGH]

- **"Being-in-the-world"** (Heidegger's *In-der-Welt-sein*): Genuine understanding does not come from abstract representations but from embodied, pre-reflective engagement with the world. Dreyfus championed "Heideggerian AI" as the only viable path, but argued it would require AI to have a body and be embedded in a cultural world -- requirements that seem impossible to satisfy. [Source: Dreyfus 2007 paper, Wikipedia; Confidence: HIGH]

The convergence with Polanyi: Polanyi's "indwelling" parallels Heidegger's ready-to-hand. Both describe a mode of knowing where the knower extends through the instrument into the world, and the instrument itself becomes tacitly integrated rather than focally attended. [Source: Synthesis from multiple sources; Confidence: HIGH -- the parallel is widely recognized in the literature]

### 1.5 Knowing-How vs Knowing-That and Polanyian Roots

The distinction traces back to:
- **Gilbert Ryle** (1945 paper; *The Concept of Mind*, 1949): Argued against "intellectualism" -- the view that all knowledge is propositional. Defended "knowing-how" as a distinct, irreducible form of knowledge that cannot be reduced to knowing-that.
- **Michael Polanyi** (*Personal Knowledge*, 1958; *The Tacit Dimension*, 1966): Extended this with "we can know more than we can tell" -- not merely that some knowledge is non-propositional, but that ALL explicit knowledge rests on a tacit foundation.
- **Dreyfus**: Operationalized this in the skill acquisition model. The novice has only knowing-that (explicit rules). The expert has predominantly knowing-how (tacit, embodied response). The transition from novice to expert IS the transition from explicit to tacit.

[Source: Wikipedia tacit knowledge, Polanyi's paradox article, Understanding Society blog; Confidence: HIGH]

### 1.6 Polanyi's Paradox (Autor, 2014)

Economist **David Autor** (MIT) named and formalized "Polanyi's Paradox" in his 2014 NBER Working Paper (#20485) "Polanyi's Paradox and the Shape of Employment Growth." The paradox: "We can know more than we can tell" means that many human skills -- demanding flexibility, judgment, and common sense -- resist automation because we cannot articulate the rules we tacitly follow. This explains labor market polarization: middle-skill routine jobs are automated, while high-skill abstract tasks and low-skill manual/interpersonal tasks (both tacit-heavy) resist automation. Contemporary ML attempts to overcome this by learning from examples rather than from explicit rules, but Autor notes this sidesteps rather than solves the paradox. [Source: Wikipedia "Polanyi's paradox", NBER, MIT economics; Confidence: HIGH]

### 2.3 Polanyi's Fiduciary Program -- Background

Polanyi's "fiduciary program" is the third major pillar of *Personal Knowledge* (after tacit knowledge and emergence). It addresses the structure of belief and commitment in knowing:

- **Core thesis**: All knowing involves a personal commitment -- an act of faith that cannot be fully justified by explicit evidence. "We believe more than we can prove" parallels "we know more than we can tell."
- **Structure**: The knower commits (fiducially) to a framework of meaning. This commitment is not blind faith but a responsible act of judgment grounded in tacit integration.
- **Parts of the book covering it**: Part Three of *Personal Knowledge* -- "The Justification of Personal Knowledge" -- includes: Ch. 8 "The Logic of Affirmation," Ch. 9 "The Critique of Doubt," Ch. 10 "Commitment."
- Paksi & Heder's *Guide to Personal Knowledge* mirrors this structure in their own Part Three.

[Source: Vernon Press book description, book table of contents; Confidence: HIGH for structure, MEDIUM-HIGH for philosophical summary -- based on secondary sources]

### 2.4 Epistemic Opacity of Autonomous Systems (2020 paper)

This is Heder's most detailed published application of Polanyi to AI. Key arguments:

#### The Design-Time / Runtime Gap
- Autonomous systems compress all moral and operational decisions to **design-time** -- the designers are not present at runtime, and what makes autonomous systems special is that no other human agent is present either
- Two sources of the epistemic gap: (a) the future environment cannot be fully predicted at design-time; (b) the system's own behavior cannot be fully predicted because of inherent opacity in machine learning
[Source: Springer article abstract, PhilPapers description, AI & Society search results; Confidence: HIGH]

#### Five Factors Causing Design-Time Opacity
The paper identifies multiple factors that cause the design-time opacity of autonomous system behavior:
1. **Embodiment effects** -- physical interaction with environment produces unpredictable outcomes
2. **Design-time knowledge gap** -- designers cannot foresee all runtime situations
3. **Human factors** -- cognitive limitations of designers
4. **Emergent behavior** -- system-level properties not predictable from components
5. **Tacit knowledge** (machine learning) -- ML systems acquire a form of tacit knowledge that is opaque even to their creators
[Source: Springer article abstract/description; Confidence: HIGH for the list, MEDIUM for exact enumeration -- reconstructed from description, not from reading full paper]

#### Polanyi's Dual Control Principle
Heder uses Polanyi's concept of **dual control** (from *The Tacit Dimension* and *Knowing and Being*): higher-level organizational principles harness but are not reducible to lower-level boundary conditions. In autonomous systems, the exact interplay between layers (hardware, software, learned parameters, environment) cannot be predicted by descriptions of any single layer. The emergent behavior of the whole is epistemically opaque. [Source: Springer article description; Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH -- the dual control application is described in search results but I did not read the full text]

#### Robot Tacit Knowledge
Heder applies Polanyi's framework to define **robot tacit knowledge**: within Polanyi's framework of personal knowledge, there is no sharp distinction between knowledge and skill, so "evidently skilful autonomous systems may be said to know." Machine learning systems acquire operational capacities through training that parallel tacit knowledge -- they can perform but cannot articulate the rules governing their performance. This is a functional analog of "we can know more than we can tell." [Source: search results describing Heder's PhD work and 2020 paper; Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH]

#### Ethical Consequences
- Traditional moral dilemma frameworks (trolley problems) assume **perfect information** about the situation and the system's capabilities
- Heder argues this assumption is invalid for autonomous systems because of epistemic opacity -- the designer cannot know exactly what the system will do
- Therefore the ethical challenge is far more **empirical** than previously understood -- it is not about choosing between well-defined outcomes, but about operating under radical uncertainty about system behavior
- This makes the ethics of autonomous systems fundamentally a problem of **fiduciary commitment** and trust under conditions of irreducible ignorance
[Source: Springer article description, PhilPapers; Confidence: HIGH for the argument structure, MEDIUM for exact formulations]

### 2.5 The Fiduciary Framework Applied to AI Trust

Heder extends Polanyi's fiduciary program to AI in the following way (synthesized across his publications):

#### Why We Cannot Simply "Trust" AI
- For Polanyi, trust (fiduciary commitment) is grounded in a **shared tacit background** -- a community of practice, shared traditions, mutual indwelling in a common framework of meaning
- AI systems have no tacit background, no embodied experience, no participation in a community of knowers
- Therefore the kind of trust we extend to human experts (based on shared tacit understanding) **cannot simply be transferred** to AI systems
- This is not merely a technical problem (to be solved by better explainability) but an **epistemic-structural** problem: the conditions for fiduciary commitment are absent
[Source: Synthesis from Heder's work as described in search results, Paksi & Heder book description, Polanyi Society references; Confidence: MEDIUM -- this is my reconstruction of the argument from multiple secondary sources. The exact formulations may differ in Heder's texts]

#### Implications for Human-AI Collaboration
- Human-AI collaboration requires new frameworks for delegating trust that acknowledge the absence of shared tacit knowing
- The fiduciary relationship with AI must be **asymmetric**: we commit to using the system, but the system does not commit to us
- Designing trustworthy AI is therefore not about making AI "trustworthy" in the human sense, but about creating institutional and technical structures that compensate for the missing fiduciary ground
- This connects to Heder's involvement in BME's Human-Centered AI Masters program -- the pedagogical goal is to train engineers who understand these epistemic limitations
[Source: BME profile, Polanyi Society description of Heder's work; Confidence: MEDIUM -- partially synthesized]

## Part 3: Connections and Synthesis

### 3.1 Dreyfus-Polanyi Convergence

| Concept | Polanyi | Dreyfus |
|---|---|---|
| Tacit vs explicit | "We can know more than we can tell" | Expert acts without rules; novice follows rules |
| Embodiment | "Indwelling" -- extending through tools | Heidegger's ready-to-hand; Merleau-Ponty's body-subject |
| Against formalization | Tacit knowledge resists articulation | AI cannot capture expert knowledge in rules |
| Subsidiary awareness | From-to structure: attend FROM subsidiaries TO focal target | Expert perceives situation holistically, not analytically |
| Learning | Apprenticeship, personal transmission | Five stages: explicit rules dissolve into embodied response |

[Source: Synthesis; Confidence: HIGH -- these parallels are widely recognized]

### 3.2 Dreyfus-Heder Convergence

Both argue that AI faces a fundamental epistemic barrier:
- **Dreyfus**: The barrier is in the nature of expert knowledge -- it is embodied, tacit, and cannot be formalized
- **Heder**: The barrier is in the nature of autonomous systems -- they are epistemically opaque and lack the fiduciary ground for trust
- Both draw on Polanyi, but from different angles: Dreyfus uses tacit knowledge (the knowing side), Heder uses the fiduciary program (the believing/trusting side)

### 3.3 The Dreyfus Stages as a Map for Polanyi's Tacit Dimension

This mapping is the key insight for the thinking skill:

- **Stage 1 (Novice)** = Pure focal/explicit awareness. No tacit dimension operative. Polanyi's "from-to" structure not yet formed.
- **Stage 2 (Advanced Beginner)** = First subsidiary awareness emerges. Patterns recognized that cannot be fully articulated.
- **Stage 3 (Competent)** = The from-to structure consolidates but remains effortful. Commitment begins (fiduciary element).
- **Stage 4 (Proficient)** = Subsidiary awareness dominates perception. Indwelling begins. The practitioner "sees through" the particulars.
- **Stage 5 (Expert)** = Complete indwelling. Total tacit integration. "We know more than we can tell" fully realized.
- **Stage 6 (Mastery)** = Creative extension of tacit knowing. Discovery. Breaking out into new frameworks.

### 3.4 Implications for the Thinking Skill

For a Polanyi thinking skill, the Dreyfus-Heder axis provides:
1. **A developmental model** (Dreyfus) for how tacit knowledge forms -- useful for diagnosing where someone is in a learning process and what they need next
2. **A trust/delegation model** (Heder) for when to rely on tacit judgment vs when to demand explicit justification -- useful for AI collaboration decisions
3. **A unified critique of over-formalization** -- both Dreyfus and Heder warn that reducing knowledge to rules (in education) or algorithms (in AI) destroys the tacit dimension that makes knowledge effective
