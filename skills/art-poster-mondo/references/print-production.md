# Screen-print production planning

Use these as provisional art-direction defaults. Confirm final tolerances, color system, mesh, halftone frequency, trapping, bleed, and file format with the selected printer.

## Format and viewing

- Start with 24 × 36 inches for a classic collectible one-sheet or 18 × 24 inches for a smaller edition; change the format when the property or venue calls for it.
- Set the trim, bleed, safe area, frame overlap, and paper deckle with the printer before final art.
- Test the design at three distances:
  - 3 m: silhouette and dominant contrast;
  - 1 m: title and main relation;
  - 30 cm: texture, references, and line craft.
- Proof a thumbnail before approving detail.

## Color strategy

- Begin with four to eight spot colors as a useful creative constraint, not a rule.
- Add colors only when they create necessary depth, material, light, or separation. Mondo examples also reach 9–12 colors.
- Include paper color as an active part of the palette.
- Reuse each ink across multiple areas when possible.
- Specify intended overprints, transparent inks, metallics, fluorescents, glow, or varnish explicitly.
- Provide a labeled composite and a swatch list with color references. Do not rely on an RGB screen preview as the contract.

## Separation

- Keep every ink on its own named layer in print order.
- Supply each separation as a solid mask or printer-approved halftone.
- Plan light-to-dark order as a baseline, then revise for opacity and intended overprint.
- Add trapping where adjacent colors could reveal registration gaps; confirm the amount with the printer.
- Keep registration marks and production notes outside trim.
- Inspect isolated plates for islands, hairline fragments, accidental transparency, and text knocked out of multiple inks.
- Rasterize only when required. Preserve vector type and hard-edged geometry where practical.

## Line and halftone

- Test the smallest positive line, reversed line, dot, and type on the chosen paper and mesh.
- Let the printer set or approve halftone frequency, angle, dot shape, and tonal floor.
- Avoid delicate tonal transitions that depend on perfect registration across several plates.
- Use texture as printable mark-making, not as an uncontrolled full-color raster overlay.

## Typography

- Keep the title, venue/date, billing, legal copy, and marks on separate editable layers.
- Provide exact copy as text in addition to outlined art.
- Check title legibility at thumbnail size.
- Avoid extremely small reversed type and thin multi-plate registration.
- Secure approval for official logos, billing order, likenesses, and legal lines before final separation.

## Paper and finish

- Choose stock for color, tooth, stiffness, ink holdout, and archival intent.
- Use off-white or kraft stock only when its cast supports the concept.
- Use foil, metallic, fluorescent, glow, or varnish only when the story explains the effect.
- Request drawdowns or a press proof for unfamiliar ink–paper combinations.

## Regular and variant editions

- Keep one core concept and hierarchy.
- Change the emotional register through palette, paper, ink behavior, or one controlled reveal.
- Explain why the variant exists in one sentence.
- Reject a variant that is merely “more expensive looking.”

## Delivery package

Provide:

- layered master file;
- outlined print file plus live-text copy deck;
- numbered color separations in print order;
- composite proof;
- dimensions, trim, bleed, and safe-area diagram;
- ink and paper specification;
- notes for overprint, trapping, halftone, and finishes;
- regular/variant comparison, if applicable;
- rights and approval status;
- thumbnail, 100% crop, and framed-view proofs.

Do not label generated raster art “print ready” until separations, typography, resolution, and printer requirements have been verified.
