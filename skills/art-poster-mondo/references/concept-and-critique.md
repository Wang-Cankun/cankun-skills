## Concept card

For each route, provide:

1. **Name:** two to five words.
2. **Poster argument:** one sentence.
3. **3 m:** dominant silhouette, field, or contrast.
4. **1 m:** title relationship and secondary narrative.
5. **30 cm:** two or three details that reward a fan.
6. **Composition:** focal point, eye path, crop, negative space, title zone.
7. **Palette:** paper color plus proposed inks; state regular/variant difference.
8. **Type:** role, era logic, weight, width, placement, and legibility risk.
9. **Technique:** illustration, collage, geometry, engraving, brush, halftone, or other property-fit process.
10. **Risk:** originality, likeness, rights, cultural context, generation, or print risk.

## Divergence test

Reject a set of routes when:

- all three use the same central figure or camera angle;
- only the palette and texture change;
- each route depends on the same iconic prop;
- each route maps to “retro poster” without a source-specific reason;
- one route is clearly developed while the others are filler.

Make each route answer a different question:

- What single idea can encode the whole premise?
- What place or object remembers the story?
- What relationship creates the emotional voltage?
- What formal tradition belongs to this world?
- What contradiction would make a fan look twice?

## 100-point rubric

| Category | Points | Full-credit standard |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Source truth | 15 | Captures the property’s emotional and thematic core without factual error |
| Fresh angle | 20 | Adds a defensible interpretation and avoids exhausted key-art formulas |
| Concept clarity | 15 | Communicates one strong idea at thumbnail and room distance |
| Hierarchy and composition | 15 | Controls focal point, eye path, negative space, and title zone |
| Visual-language fit | 10 | Chooses form, technique, and reference tradition because they suit the property |
| Typography | 10 | Makes the title integral, readable, era-aware, and technically plausible |
| Print/output feasibility | 10 | Supports separations, scale, crop, registration, and accurate final text |
| Collectible finish | 5 | Uses paper, ink, variant, and close-view detail with narrative purpose |
| **Total** | **100** | **Require 80+, with no category below half its points** |

Apply two unscored gates:

- **Integrity:** no copied composition, false affiliation, or unsupported licensing claim.
- **Authorship:** no request to imitate the exact style of a named living artist.

When reporting a numeric total, show all eight category scores and add them directly. Do not normalize a subtotal from an abbreviated table to 100. For a compact route comparison, use qualitative labels or clearly mark the numbers as partial and omit a total.

## Diagnostic order

Critique in this order:

1. **Idea:** Is the poster argument specific and fresh?
2. **Read:** What is seen first, second, and third?
3. **Source:** Which detail proves knowledge of the work?
4. **Type:** Does title placement strengthen the image?
5. **Craft:** Are drawing, color, texture, and effects coherent?
6. **Object:** Does the print plan add meaning?

Do not recommend polishing texture when the idea or hierarchy is still weak.

## Frequent failure modes

- **Promotional collage:** many approved faces, no interpretation.
- **Generic minimalism:** one object on empty space with no second idea.
- **Unmotivated retro:** period type, grain, and muted inks unrelated to the title.
- **Easter-egg soup:** trivia replaces hierarchy.
- **Premium gimmick:** foil or glow competes with the image.
- **Title afterthought:** artwork leaves no stable typographic zone.
- **Fake screen print:** full photographic gradients are merely overlaid with noise.
- **Reference dependency:** the poster only works when placed beside the work it copies.
- **Variant drift:** the variant becomes a different concept instead of another emotional register.

## Generation-prompt architecture

Write prompts in this order:

1. original poster argument;
2. dominant composition and camera relation;
3. focal subject and action;
4. environmental or symbolic mechanism;
5. hierarchy and negative-space reservation;
6. limited palette with paper color and ink count;
7. line, shape, texture, and print behavior;
8. aspect ratio and crop;
9. exact exclusions.

Use exclusions such as:

> no existing poster composition, no Mondo logo, no artist signature, no mock title text, no billing text, no generic floating-head montage, no photoreal marketing key art, no arbitrary distressed border

Describe high-level design properties instead of naming a living artist. Generate the illustration base first; add exact words separately.

## Revision prescription

Return at most five changes, ordered by impact:

1. replace or sharpen the poster argument;
2. repair the dominant silhouette and scale relations;
3. simplify or redirect the eye path;
4. integrate title and required copy;
5. tune palette, texture, separation, and finish.

Rescore the proposed revision and identify the single remaining risk.
