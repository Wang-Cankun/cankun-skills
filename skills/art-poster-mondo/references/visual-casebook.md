## Martin Ansin — *Metropolis* (2009)

### Published facts

- Created for an SXSW screening with live score.
- Produced as a 24 × 36 inch screen print.
- Used metallic silver inks and glow ink in the regular version; the gold variant used metallic gold inks and had a run of 30.

Sources:

- [Mondo official release](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/16990663-sxsw-metropolis-posters)
- [Slashfilm contemporary report and images](https://www.slashfilm.com/502771/cool-stuff-martin-ansins-metropolis-poster/)

### Visual observations

- Give the robot Maria’s cropped face monumental scale while compressing the human cast and tower into the opposing field.
- Use heavy diagonals as mechanical structure rather than as generic action lines.
- Make the title geometry participate in the architecture.
- Let silver versus gold shift the reading from cold machine to icon or relic.

### Transferable lesson

Integrate title, structure, and finish into one world-specific system. Use glow only as a verified second viewing state; do not invent what an undocumented hidden layer reveals.

## Olly Moss — *Star Wars* trilogy (2010)

### Published facts

- Three 24 × 36 inch prints, each in an edition of 400.
- WIRED describes the silhouettes of C-3PO, Boba Fett, and Darth Vader containing landscape and story details.
- Moss rewatched the films and sketched ideas; the Mondo team described itself as “fans first.”
- Mondo used metallic- and glow-ink samples while pursuing Lucasfilm approval.

Sources:

- [WIRED interview and complete images](https://www.wired.com/2010/12/olly-moss-star-wars-mondo/)
- [Mondo, *The Art of Mondo*](https://mondoshop.com/products/the-art-of-mondo)

### Visual observations

- Create a double image: an instantly recognizable silhouette at distance and a narrative world inside it at close range.
- Unify a series through format, paper field, title zone, and color logic.
- Let one dominant color distinguish each installment without breaking the set.
- Compress copy into a stable lower field so illustration and typography read as one collection.

### Transferable lesson

Use “one icon, two readings” when a franchise needs both immediate recognition and fan-level discovery. Do not copy the silhouette-container arrangement itself.

## Tyler Stout — *Akira* (2011)

### Published facts

- A 36 × 24 inch signed and numbered screen print with a regular edition of 180 and a variant of 80.
- Stout says the explosion and Kaneda’s motorcycle were central from the beginning.
- He drew people as separate puzzle pieces before assembling the composition.
- He described it as his highest-color-count Mondo job at the time.

Sources:

- [Mondo Mystery Movie record](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/16991999-first-ever-mondo-mystery-movie)
- [Film on Paper interview with Tyler Stout](https://www.filmonpaper.com/blog/an-interview-with-tyler-stout-on-the-creation-of-his-akira-print/)

### Visual observations

- Use several scales—large face, full figure, vehicle, title, and smaller cast—to keep a dense field hierarchical.
- Repeat red through clothing, violence, energy, and title to create an eye path.
- Use borders and a grounded title block to contain visual pressure.
- Reward repeat viewing without allowing secondary figures to erase the central explosion.

### Transferable lesson

Treat maximalism as scale choreography, not as accumulation. Keep components movable through revision.

## Aaron Horkey — *Jurassic Park* (2011)

### Published facts

- A 21 × 39 inch, 10-color screen print.
- Production used five split-fountain gradients and two metallic inks.
- The regular edition of 220 and variant of 75 used different French paper stocks.

Sources:

- [Mondo official archive](https://mondoshop.com/products/jurassic-park-aaron-horkey-poster)
- [Burlesque of North America production record](https://burlesquedesign.com/products/jurrasic-park)
- [WIRED contemporary report](https://www.wired.com/2011/09/mondo-jurassic-park/)

### Visual observations

- Use an unusually narrow vertical format to make the creature monumental.
- Combine natural-history engraving, ornamental framing, and hand lettering with a modern film property.
- Integrate the title into the border rather than treating it as a replaceable overlay.
- Let warm and cool variants change the creature from fossilized myth to active predator.

### Transferable lesson

Choose a historical print language when it deepens the subject. Make the variant alter mood, not merely hue.

## Laurent Durieux — *Jaws* (2013)

### Published facts

- A 24 × 36 inch screen print, edition of 525, printed by D&L Screenprinting.
- Durieux says he could not surpass the original poster and lacked most portrait rights.
- He therefore chose the opposite direction: no blood, no shark, and a sunny, innocent holiday image.
- Mondo later noted that the poster caught Steven Spielberg’s attention.

Sources:

- [Mondo official product page](https://mondoshop.com/products/jaws-laurent-durieux-poster)
- [Mondo detail release](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/16992755-laurent-durieuxs-jaws-detail-shots)
- [Laurent Durieux interview](https://eviltender.com/2013/03/05/making-art-the-beauty-design-of-laurent-durieux/)
- [Mondo retrospective](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/mondo-gallery-presents-the-art-of-laurent-durieux-part-two)

### Visual observations

- Put ordinary beach life and a large foreground umbrella where viewers expect a monster.
- Let the viewer’s memory supply danger to an apparently safe postcard.
- Use promotional language and calm composition as dramatic irony.
- Keep the title subordinate enough for the environment to carry the story.

### Transferable lesson

Search for “another accurate truth” about the film. Omission can create more tension than depiction. Treat readings such as “the umbrella resembles a fin” as observations unless the artist confirms them.

## Rory Kurtz — *Parasite* (2020)

### Published facts

- A 24 × 36 inch screen print by D&L Screenprinting.
- The regular version was a timed edition; the Korean-language variant had an edition of 325.
- Kurtz states that the wealthy family occupies a perfect moment while remaining unaware, and that the poorer family is compressed into shadow.

Sources:

- [Mondo release and artist statement](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/new-parasite-timed-edition-variant-by-rory-kurtz)
- [Mondo regular-edition page](https://mondoshop.com/products/parasite-timed-edition)
- [Rory Kurtz project page](https://www.rorykurtz.com/parasite)

### Visual observations

- Make the house an explicit class diagram by placing one family above and another physically below.
- Use the bitten peach as a fan-specific detail while preserving the architectural thesis.
- Let the title act as a spatial divider instead of floating over the image.
- Use language change in a variant to deepen cultural and collectible meaning.

### Transferable lesson

Allow likeness-based art when the arrangement itself expresses the theme. Portraits are not the problem; a portrait inventory without a spatial argument is.

## Hans Woody — *Dune: Part Two* versions 1 and 2 (2025)

### Published facts

- Both are 36 × 24 inch, eight-color screen prints by D&L Screenprinting, each in an edition of 190.
- Version 1 uses gold foil paper and version 2 blue foil paper.
- The artist describes the pair as Fremen and Harkonnen stories with contrasting colorways.
- Mondo’s art-direction note links the work to 1970s science-fiction landscape traditions.

Sources:

- [Mondo release and statements](https://mondoshop.com/blogs/news/dune-part-two-by-hans-woody-posters-on-sale-info)
- [Mondo version 1](https://mondoshop.com/products/dune-part-two-version-1-poster)
- [Mondo version 2](https://mondoshop.com/products/dune-part-two-version-2-poster)

### Visual observations

- Use paired landscape stages to make rival factions legible as two environments.
- Keep figures subordinate to monumental world scale.
- Convert gold and blue foil from premium effects into faction-specific light.
- Let the two prints complete one conflict rather than treating one as a simple recolor.

### Transferable lesson

Use a diptych when the property contains a true dual structure. Make substrate and palette carry worldbuilding.

## Cross-case archetypes

| Archetype | Cases | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Double image or negative-space icon | *Star Wars* | Immediate recognition plus hidden narrative |
| Controlled maximal ensemble | *Akira* | World scale, cast energy, and repeated discovery |
| Quiet environmental irony | *Jaws* | Suspense through omission and audience memory |
| Decorative print or natural-history object | *Jurassic Park* | Mythic weight and handcrafted collectibility |
| Architectural or spatial metaphor | *Parasite* | Express theme through physical arrangement |
| Integrated type-as-world | *Metropolis*, *Jurassic Park* | Make title and frame part of the fiction |
| Paired landscape or faction diptych | *Dune: Part Two* | Present a conflict as two coherent worlds |

Choose one primary archetype. Combine a second only when it clarifies rather than decorates the idea.

## Design conclusions

- Design the “second poster”: assume the viewer knows the property and reward interpretation after viewing.
- Aim for one strong distant read and several accurate close-view discoveries.
- Match visual tradition, typography, and drawing method to the property instead of defaulting to “retro.”
- Decide metallic, glow, split-fountain, foil, or variant logic during concept development.
- Use title design as part of the image’s world.
- Make variants change state, faction, language, substrate, or emotional register.
- Avoid generic cast piles, copied famous frames, invented easter eggs, and post-hoc premium finishes.
